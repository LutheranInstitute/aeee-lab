\begin{abstract}
Human--agent systems today are \emph{capacitive}: they scale the number of agents
one person can supervise, but not the \emph{authority} those agents may exercise.
We argue that the missing layer is not infrastructure but the product itself --- a
\emph{conductor}: an intermediate process through which one human directs many
agents, and through which $N$ receipts are consolidated into one verified truth. We
specify the conductor's five duties (translate, arbitrate, fan out, fan in, hold
memory) and identify \emph{fan-in} --- consolidating $N$ receipts into a single
verified truth --- as the unsolved gap. We then extend the model vertically into a
four-layer stack: \emph{sovereign} (cryptographic custody and authority),
\emph{private client} (local-first surface), \emph{digital twin} (conductor
memory), and \emph{digital human} (voice and face). We observe that recent
full-duplex speech models such as NVIDIA PersonaPlex, and digital-human pipelines
such as NVIDIA ACE/Tokkio, solve the \emph{projection} layer --- voice, face, and
natural turn-taking --- while providing no cryptographic identity. We show that
this missing layer is exactly what a sovereign signing substrate supplies, and we
propose treating every full-duplex conversational act as a \emph{signed dispatch}
that passes the same gate as any other action. We give a minimal specification of
the receipt rail and report a working instance (5 waves, 20 tasks, 20/20 verified).
Our central claim: NVIDIA provides the mirror; the sovereign layer owns the thing
mirrored and the right to reflect it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A one-person company is not a company with one employee. It is a company whose
\emph{authority} does not scale with headcount. The founder cannot hire, cannot
delegate custody, and cannot personally verify the work of twenty agents at once.
Yet the tools available --- LLM APIs, agent frameworks, orchestration platforms ---
are all \emph{capacitive}: they increase throughput without increasing authority.

We contend that the layer between the human and the fleet is not plumbing. It is
the product. We call it \ae://, and this paper specifies it.

Two claims organise the paper. First, that the correct abstraction is a
\emph{conductor}, and that the conductor's hard problem is not dispatch (fan-out)
but \emph{consolidation} (fan-in): turning $N$ agent receipts into one verified
truth. Second, that the same authority substrate that makes fan-in trustworthy is
what a \emph{digital human} needs and does not have --- and that a full-duplex
conversational model is, structurally, a stream of \emph{actions} that must pass
the same gate.

\section{The Conductor Model}

\subsection{Definition}

Let $H$ be a single human principal and $A=\{a_1,\dots,a_N\}$ a set of $N$
autonomous agents. A conductor $C$ is an intermediate process such that
\begin{align}
  H &\rightarrow C && \text{(intent)} \\
  C &\rightarrow a_i && \text{(dispatch)} \\
  a_i &\rightarrow C && \text{(receipt)} \\
  C &\rightarrow H && \text{(consolidated truth)}
\end{align}
$C$ is the only process that both issues and receives. The human never addresses
an agent directly; an agent never reports to the human directly. Identity is
\emph{relational}: an agent is defined by its position in this graph, not by an
intrinsic property.

\subsection{The Five Duties}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Translate.} Convert natural-language intent into typed dispatches:
        a capability address, a task specification, and an authority scope.
  \item \textbf{Arbitrate.} Every dispatch must be authorised by a capability.
        Custody of the signing key remains with $H$. The conductor may
        \emph{propose}; it may not \emph{approve}.
  \item \textbf{Fan out.} Partition work into \emph{waves} --- batches dispatched
        in parallel across the fleet.
  \item \textbf{Fan in.} Collect receipts from each agent and consolidate them
        into a single verified truth. \emph{This is the critical gap.}
  \item \textbf{Hold memory.} Maintain a digest --- a self-referencing record of
        what was dispatched, completed, and outstanding --- across instances.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Gap: Fan-In}

Fan-out is largely solved: partition, dispatch, parallelise. Fan-in is not. The
consolidation of $N$ receipts into one truth requires
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Addressability}: each receipt cites the capability address that
        produced it;
  \item \textbf{Verifiability}: each receipt carries a signature checkable against
        that capability's public key;
  \item \textbf{Deduplication}: where several agents attempt the same task, select
        one winner;
  \item \textbf{Consolidation}: reduce to a single boolean, \textsc{pass} or
        \textsc{fail}.
\end{itemize}
We call this structure the \textbf{receipt rail}. Without it, the conductor is a
load balancer, not a conductor.

\section{The Vertical Stack}

The conductor sits atop a four-layer stack:
\begin{align}
  \textsc{digital human}  &\quad \text{(voice $+$ face) --- the mirror} \notag\\
  \textsc{digital twin}   &\quad \text{(conductor memory) --- the thing mirrored} \notag\\
  \textsc{private client} &\quad \text{(local-first surface) --- where the twin lives} \notag\\
  \textsc{sovereign}      &\quad \text{(custody $+$ authority) --- who may act} \notag
\end{align}
Each layer depends on the one below: the human cannot speak without the twin's
memory; the twin cannot remember without the client's storage; the client cannot
store without the sovereign's custody.

\subsection{Sovereign}
The sovereign layer holds an Ed25519 signing key and is the only layer that can
authorise an action. The human is its sole custodian. The conductor may request a
signature; the sovereign may refuse.

\subsection{Private Client}
The private client is the local-first interface --- the human's name, logo, and
configuration, and the place the twin resides when not projected. It is personal in
the legal sense: authority and credentials are never exported.

\subsection{Digital Twin}
The twin is the conductor's persistent memory, \emph{feed-informed}: it updates as
receipts arrive and supplies the digital human's responses. It is the record of
what the human intended and what was done.

\subsection{Digital Human}
The digital human is the projection. Recent full-duplex speech models make this
layer newly tractable: NVIDIA \emph{PersonaPlex}~\cite{personaplex} is a
$7$B full-duplex speech-to-speech model, built on
\emph{Moshi}~\cite{moshi}, that allows voice conditioning and role control through
a text prompt, and that handles interruptions, backchannels, and turn-taking with
reported latencies near $0.17$\,s (turn-taking) and $0.24$\,s (interruption).
NVIDIA \emph{ACE}/Tokkio~\cite{ace,tokkio} supplies the 3D animation pipeline
(Audio2Face). Together these provide voice, face, and presence. They do not provide
cryptographic identity.

\section{The Gate: Full-Duplex as Signed Acts}

\subsection{The Problem}
A full-duplex model listens and speaks simultaneously, interrupting, yielding, and
backchannelling in real time. Each such behaviour is an \emph{act} --- a change to
the shared state of a conversation. In a single-agent system an act is cheap. In a
conductor system, an act by the digital human is an act by \emph{all agents the
human represents}, and must therefore pass the gate.

\subsection{The Proposal}
We propose that every full-duplex act be treated as a dispatch:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item the twin generates a candidate act (speak, pause, yield, interrupt);
  \item the conductor validates it against the human's current intent and scope;
  \item the sovereign signs the act as a \texttt{sign\_card} capability;
  \item the act is projected through the digital human;
  \item a receipt is written to the rail.
\end{enumerate}
This is not throttling; it is \emph{authority enforcement}. The digital human may
speak freely --- but only for the conductor, and only within the conductor's scope.

\subsection{The Canonical Separation}
\begin{quote}
\textbf{NVIDIA provides the mirror; \ae:// owns the thing mirrored and the right to
reflect it.}
\end{quote}
The mirror is a solved problem. The authority to reflect --- to decide whose face
may speak, and what it may say --- is not.

\section{The Receipt Rail: A Minimal Specification}

The rail is a single JSON document, versioned by schema:
\begin{verbatim}
{
  "schema": "receipt-rail/v1",
  "batches": [
    {"wave": "daollc-content", "status": "complete",
     "tasks": 5, "complete": 5,
     "verb_list": ["scan","extract","claim","complete","verify"]}
  ],
  "total_waves": 5, "total_tasks": 20, "total_complete": 20,
  "consolidated_verdict": "PASS - 20/20 verified via fleet waves",
  "topology": "one human -> waves (fan-out) -> agents -> receipts (fan-in)"
}
\end{verbatim}
Each batch is a \emph{wave}; each wave is a fleet with receipt tasks. A working
instance is deployed and verifies $20/20$ tasks across $5$ waves. The capability
that authorises a dispatch binds the verb, task identifier, subject, expiry, and a
single-use nonce, and is signed with Ed25519.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Full-duplex speech.} PersonaPlex~\cite{personaplex} extends
Moshi~\cite{moshi} with voice and role control; both replace the cascaded
ASR$\rightarrow$LLM$\rightarrow$TTS pipeline with a single dual-stream model. We
adopt this class as the projection layer and observe that it has no notion of
authority.

\paragraph{Digital humans.} NVIDIA ACE~\cite{ace} and the Tokkio reference
workflow~\cite{tokkio} compose NIM microservices (Riva ASR/TTS, Audio2Face, an
LLM) into a lifelike avatar. The blueprint is enterprise-facing and
identity-agnostic.

\paragraph{Mixture of agents.} MoA~\cite{moa} improves generation by combining
models. We extend the idea from generation to \emph{execution}: dispatching and
verifying actions, with a signed receipt as the unit of trust.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Hardware.} The reference environment (NVIDIA RTX 3050, 6\,GB, cc
        8.6) cannot hold PersonaPlex-7B in bf16 ($\approx 14$\,GB). A $q4_k$
        quantisation ($\approx 4.5$\,GB) is the only plausible local path and has
        not been measured; the ACE/Tokkio blueprint targets datacenter GPUs.
  \item \textbf{Gate latency.} Signing every conversational act adds latency whose
        effect on naturalness we have not measured.
  \item \textbf{Cross-instance fan-in.} The reported rail consolidates within a
        single instance; consolidation across eight instances is future work.
  \item \textbf{Proposed vs.\ measured.} The conductor and stack are specified;
        the receipt rail is deployed and verified ($20/20$); the gated full-duplex
        digital human is proposed.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

The one-person company is limited not by compute but by authority. The conductor,
not the agent, is the product. We specified five duties, identified fan-in as the
critical gap, gave a minimal receipt rail, and extended the model vertically to a
digital human. The missing layer there is cryptographic identity --- which the
sovereign layer supplies. The mirror is solved. The right to reflect is not.
